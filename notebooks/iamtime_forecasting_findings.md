# iAmTime forecasting demos: findings across four fev-bench domains

**Scope.** Public checkpoint `anishsaha/iAmTime-base-forecast`, CPU, four notebooks: Walmart (weekly store×dept sales, 39-week horizon), Rossmann (daily store sales, 48 days), Energy (ERCOT hourly load, ETT transformer, EPF day-ahead prices, ENTSO-e daily load), Econ (US consumption, FRED-MD, GVAR). Every claim below is backed by a scorecard on 60–512 forecasts with win rate and paired-bootstrap CI; MASE is per-series, scaled by the in-sample (seasonal) naive error, so values are comparable only within a row.

## Headline results

## What the ICL experiments establish

- **The model reads the history → future mapping, not just extra context.** Shuffled-futures controls are 1.5–6× worse than zero-shot (Walmart 2.95, ENTSO-e 6.0, GDP 8.3 vs ~1.0–2.8). The one exception (Rossmann, control still beats zero-shot) is explained by all futures sharing the same Christmas pattern.
- **Selection matters more than count.** Hierarchy-based choices (same department in other stores; the series' own earlier years; same store type) beat k-means clustering, which was no better than random series. k-shot curves: most of the gain by k = 3–5, flat after; k > 6 (beyond training) neither helps nor breaks the model.
- **Examples substitute for missing seasonality, not for a long history.** With the full 104-week Walmart history, zero-shot (0.78) still beats the best ICL variant on a 52-week query (0.90).

## Shortcomings to flag

1. **Covariates inside examples disable the query's own covariate response.** With any promotion covariate in the examples, Walmart predicted uplift collapses to ≈0 at every strength (ratio 0.03–0.05), even with a single 1x example matching the history. Root cause unknown (checkpoint vs. pipeline input handling). This blocks the strongest unique claim, "examples teach a covariate → response rule", and all later notebooks avoid example covariates.
2. **Wrong examples are catastrophic, so ICL needs a reliable selector.** Season-matched examples from arbitrary departments made Walmart's 12-week cold start *worse* on the mean (1.55 vs 1.33); analogues on noisy, mean-reverting series (inflation) hurt; with too little query history (8 quarters) similarity search degenerates to random. ICL is safe only with a structural selector (hierarchy, calendar, own past).
3. **Multivariate input is not an advantage to advertise.** No reliable gain in 3 of 4 settings and a significant loss on near-collinear variates; present it as "robust to joint input" rather than "better".
4. **Conservative extrapolation.** Covariate responses shrink toward the range seen in history (2x/3x promotions under-predicted by 20–33 %), and sensitivities run below historical regressions. Fine for interpolation and ranking scenarios, not for absolute uplift outside the observed range.
5. **Past-only covariates contribute little** (one test, GVAR); gains come from known-future inputs.
6. **Macro regime changes** (rate cuts, COVID-year levels) are not anticipated with or without covariates; the model behaves like a well-calibrated trend/seasonal extrapolator.

## One-line summary

iAmTime zero-shot beats the standard baselines in retail, energy and macro; known-future covariates give the largest and most reliable gains (up to −43 % MASE on Rossmann, −16 % on EPF) with plausible what-if behaviour; in-context examples deliver real, control-validated gains of **11–25 % lower mean MASE** for short-history and seasonal-horizon queries (Rossmann Christmas −25 %, ENTSO-e −20 %, Walmart −12 %, GDP −11 %) and **14–25 %** for cold starts with 12–14 periods of history (Rossmann −25 %, Walmart −20 %, ENTSO-e −14 %), with 56–99 % win rates. The optimum is **3–5 structurally matched examples** (own earlier years, siblings): the first 1–3 deliver most of the gain, the curve is flat by k ≈ 5, and going to 12 adds at most 1–3 % and never hurts, so more examples are not where the value is; the selector is. Open issues: example covariates break the query's covariate response, multivariate input gives no dependable gain, and covariate effects are under-extrapolated outside the historical range.
